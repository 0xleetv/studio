A total solar eclipse

The sky's rarest show is a coincidence of sizes.

Geometry 01 · Coincidence

Total

Full cover. Daylight collapses.

Approach · First contact

A slow takeover

The moon swallows the sun slowly. At 3,700 kilometres per hour it still takes more than an hour to finish the job, and the light dies a little at a time.

Totality

The corona

For those few minutes the sun shows the one thing daylight always hides. The corona, silver-white, rippling in solar wind. Its shape follows the sun's magnetic field, and it never looks the same twice.

Totality · The chill

The air changes

The air cools by whole degrees. Birds go silent and head for roost, tricked by night at noon. Every horizon glows at once, twilight running a full circle.

Light 02 · The last bead

Diamond ring

One last bead of sunlight threads a valley on the moon's rim and burns like a diamond ring. Francis Baily drew it in 1836.

Nightfall · The umbra

Darkness lands

The umbra does not creep in. It slams past at thousands of kilometres per hour, a ribbon of night a few hundred kilometres wide. Stand outside it and you see nothing at all.

Stars at midday

Midnight noon

Venus steps out of a noon sky. The brightest stars follow. Every horizon burns like sunset, because the light is leaving everywhere except the circle you stand in. In 1919, photographs taken in totality showed starlight bending around the sun.

Geometry 03 · The shadow

Path centre

One shadow races across the face of the Earth. Where you stand decides everything. Inside the umbra the sky is dark enough for the corona. One step outside, and it is day.

Light 05 · The paradox

1,000,000 °C

The corona burns a hundred times hotter than the surface beneath it. Nobody has fully explained why.
